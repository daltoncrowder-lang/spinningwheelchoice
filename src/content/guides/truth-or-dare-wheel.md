## Why a wheel makes the game better

The slowest part of truth or dare is waiting while someone thinks of a question. A prepared wheel removes that pause. It also stops anyone from targeting one player with harsh prompts, because the wheel picks, not the person asking.

## Two ways to set it up

**One wheel:** a simple two-slice wheel with "Truth" and "Dare." Players pick their own prompts from a list you agreed on.

**Two wheels:** one wheel for truth or dare, plus a wheel of prompts for each. This is faster and works well on a shared screen at parties.

## Clean truth ideas

- What's the most embarrassing song on your playlist?
- What's a food you pretend to like?
- What's the strangest thing you believed as a kid?
- If you could swap lives with anyone here for a day, who and why?
- What's the last thing you searched online?

## Clean dare ideas

- Talk in an accent for the next two rounds.
- Do your best impression of someone in the room, and let them guess.
- Let the group pick your phone wallpaper for a day.
- Sing the chorus of the last song you listened to.
- Speak only in questions until your next turn.

## Ground rules that keep it fun

Agree on these before the first spin: everyone gets a set number of passes, no dares that involve danger or leaving the room, no sharing other people's secrets, and anyone can end the game. A good round of truth or dare leaves everyone laughing, not uncomfortable.
