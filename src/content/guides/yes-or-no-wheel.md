  - q: Is a yes or no wheel really 50/50?
    a: Yes, as long as both slices are the same size. Each spin picks a random position on the wheel, so two equal slices give each answer the same chance every time, no matter what came up before.
  - q: Should I add a "maybe" option?
    a: Add "maybe" or "ask again tomorrow" if you want a way out of decisions you are not ready to make. Keep in mind it changes the odds to one in three for each answer.

    a: Use it to check your feelings, not to decide. If the wheel says no and you feel disappointed, that reaction is useful information about what you want.

## When a yes or no wheel helps

Most of the decisions that eat up time are the small ones where both options are fine. Should you go to the gym tonight or tomorrow? Order pizza or cook? Watch one more episode? These choices are not worth ten minutes of back-and-forth, and a two-slice wheel settles them in about three seconds.

It also helps in groups. When four friends can't agree on whether to go out, a spin everyone agreed to in advance ends the debate without anyone having to be the bad guy.

## The coin-flip trick

There is an old idea, often credited to a few different writers, that you should flip a coin on a hard choice. Not because the coin knows the answer, but because of how you feel in the half second after it lands. If the wheel lands on "no" and your first reaction is relief, you have learned something. If it lands on "no" and you feel let down, you probably wanted "yes" all along.

That makes a yes or no wheel more useful than it sounds. It is a fast way to surface a preference you already had.

## Ways to set it up

- **Classic:** two slices, "Yes" and "No." Even odds, no ambiguity.
- **With an exit:** add "Maybe" or "Spin again tomorrow" for decisions that can wait.
- **Weighted on purpose:** if you want to nudge yourself toward a habit, use three "Yes" slices and one "No." The wheel is still fair per slice, but you control the overall odds.

## Making it stick

Decide before you spin that you will follow the result. A wheel only saves time if the answer is final. If you find yourself spinning again until you get the answer you want, that is a clear sign you already know what you want to do. Go do it.
