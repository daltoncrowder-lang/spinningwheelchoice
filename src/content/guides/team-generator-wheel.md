## Why skip team captains

Picking teams by captains is fast, but it is hard on whoever gets picked last, and it tends to put friends together. A wheel avoids both problems. Everyone sees the same random process, and the teams come out mixed.

## Step by step

1. Add every participant's name to the wheel.
2. Decide how many teams you need and number them.
3. Spin. The first name goes to Team 1, the next to Team 2, and so on in rotation.
4. Remove each name after it's picked so nobody is chosen twice.
5. Keep spinning until everyone is on a team.

If the group doesn't divide evenly, decide in advance which team gets the extra player, or let one more spin decide.

## Balancing skill without losing fairness

Pure randomness sometimes puts all the strongest players on one side. A simple fix is a two-wheel approach:

- **Wheel A:** the most experienced players or the people who know the material best.
- **Wheel B:** everyone else.

Spin Wheel A first, rotating across teams, then spin Wheel B. The teams stay random, but each one starts with a strong core.

## Where team wheels work well

- **Gym class and recess games:** no captains, no hurt feelings.
- **Work workshops:** mixes departments so people meet colleagues they don't usually work with.
- **Trivia and game nights:** keeps the same couples from always teaming up.
- **Pickup sports:** settles teams quickly so play can start.
