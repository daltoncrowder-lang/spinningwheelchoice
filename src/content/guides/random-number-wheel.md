## Wheel or generator?

Both pick numbers with equal odds. A number generator is faster when you just need a number for yourself. A wheel is better when other people need to see and trust the result. A classroom watching a wheel slow down to land on 7 accepts the outcome more readily than a number appearing on a laptop screen.

## Common ranges and what they're for

- **1 to 6:** a stand-in for a die when you've lost one from a board game.
- **1 to 10:** picking a question number from a worksheet, or rating something at random.
- **1 to 30:** calling on students by class number.
- **1 to 100:** raffles with numbered tickets, though large ranges are easier to read on a generator.

## Ideas for games and lessons

- **Math warm-ups:** spin two numbers and have students add, multiply, or compare them.
- **Exercise breaks:** spin a number of jumping jacks or seconds of plank.
- **Board game nights:** replace missing dice or decide who goes first.
- **Raffles:** match the spin to numbered tickets and remove each winning number.

## Drawing without repeats

If each number should come up only once, remove it from the wheel after each spin. That turns the wheel into a shuffled sequence, useful for presentation order, numbered seating, or bingo-style games.
