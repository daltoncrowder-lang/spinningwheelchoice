## Why random calling works

When students raise their hands, the same five usually answer. The rest learn they can drift. A visible wheel changes that, because anyone could be next. Teachers often see attention go up in the seconds before a spin, especially with younger classes who enjoy watching the wheel slow down.

It also takes the teacher out of the choice. Nobody can say you always pick on them when the wheel did the picking.

## Setting up your class wheel

1. Paste your class list into the wheel, one name per line.
2. Project the wheel on the board so the class can see the spin.
3. Ask the question first, give 10 to 20 seconds of think time, and then spin. Spinning before the question lets everyone except the chosen student relax.
4. Remove each name after it is picked if you want everyone to get a turn before anyone repeats.

## Ideas beyond calling on students

- **Line leader and classroom jobs:** spin each Monday to assign jobs for the week.
- **Review games:** put topics or question numbers on the wheel instead of names.
- **Partner work:** spin twice to make random pairs, which mixes up social groups.
- **Reading order:** decide who reads aloud next without anyone feeling singled out.

## Keeping it kind

A random wheel should lower stress, not raise it. Make "phone a friend" or "pass for now" a normal option, and come back to students who pass. The goal is that everyone expects to take part, not that anyone gets caught out.
