## Why a wheel beats a chore chart

A chart tells kids what to do. A wheel lets them watch the decision happen. That small difference matters, because the most common complaint about chores is "that's not fair." When the wheel picks, the complaint has nowhere to go. Many kids also enjoy the spin itself, which turns a chore assignment into a small event.

## Picking the right jobs

Put jobs on the wheel that each child can do without help. Mixing a five-year-old and a twelve-year-old on one wheel tends to backfire, so either make one wheel per age group or include only chores that everyone can do.

**Ages 4 to 6:** set the table, water plants, sort laundry by color, put away toys.

**Ages 7 to 10:** unload the dishwasher, sweep, take out recycling, feed pets.

**Ages 11 and up:** wash dishes, vacuum, clean a bathroom sink, fold and put away laundry.

## Running the weekly spin

1. Pick a set time, like Sunday after dinner.
2. Each child spins once per chore slot, oldest to youngest or the reverse each week.
3. Write the results somewhere visible, like the fridge.
4. Trades are allowed only if both kids agree, and anything disputed goes back to the wheel.

## Adding rewards

Some families add a "free pass" slice or a bonus job worth extra screen time. Keep rewards simple and predictable, so the wheel stays about fairness rather than turning into a game kids try to win.
