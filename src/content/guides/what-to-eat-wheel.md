## Why dinner decisions are so hard

By evening, most people have already made hundreds of small decisions. "What's for dinner" lands at the worst possible time, and the conversation goes in circles because nobody wants to pick and be blamed. A wheel breaks the loop because the choice comes from outside the group.

## Build a wheel you will actually follow

The trick is to filter before you spin, not after. If the wheel includes a meal that takes an hour or a restaurant across town, you will just spin again. Ask three quick questions first:

- **How much time do we have?** Under 30 minutes means only fast meals go on.
- **What is already in the fridge?** Add meals that use what you have so food doesn't go to waste.
- **What's the budget tonight?** Leave takeout off on nights you want to cook.

## Starter ideas

**Fast at home:** tacos, pasta with whatever vegetables you have, breakfast for dinner, rice bowls, grilled cheese and soup, stir fry.

**Low-effort nights:** frozen pizza with a salad, sandwich bar, leftovers buffet.

**Treat nights:** your favorite local takeout, a new restaurant you keep meaning to try, homemade burgers.

## Rules that keep the peace

Set the ground rules before the wheel moves. One veto each, and then the next spin is final. Kids especially like taking turns pressing the spin button, which makes them more likely to eat whatever it lands on.
